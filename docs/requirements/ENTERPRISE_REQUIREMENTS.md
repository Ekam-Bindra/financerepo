# AI-Powered Equity Research Assistant
## Enterprise Product Requirements Document

**Version:** 1.0  
**Program Duration:** 48 weeks  
**Delivery Cadence:** 24 two-week sprints  
**Product Classification:** Enterprise financial research and decision-support platform

## 1. Executive Summary

The AI-Powered Equity Research Assistant is an enterprise-grade web platform that collects publicly available corporate information, converts financial documents into structured and searchable data, applies artificial intelligence to identify material developments, and generates source-grounded equity research outputs.

The system is intended to reduce the manual effort required during the first stage of public-company research. It retrieves filings and related materials, extracts financial and qualitative information, compares reporting periods, identifies changes, supports natural-language questions, and generates professional research summaries.

The platform is not intended to replace professional analysts, portfolio managers, licensed financial professionals, or human investment judgment. All AI-generated findings must remain traceable to original sources, distinguish facts from interpretations, and communicate uncertainty.

The first enterprise release focuses on U.S. publicly traded companies and SEC-related disclosures while preserving architectural room for international filings, licensed market data, portfolio analytics, valuation tools, and collaborative research workflows.

## 2. Product Vision

Create a trusted AI research workspace that allows an analyst to enter a public-company ticker and receive a structured, source-linked, auditable view of:

- Company and operating-segment information.
- Recent financial performance.
- Historical financial trends.
- Management guidance.
- Material changes since prior reporting periods.
- Business risks and opportunities.
- Management sentiment.
- Capital-allocation activity.
- Relevant filings, presentations, releases, and transcripts.
- AI-generated summaries and research reports.
- Answers to company-specific financial questions.

## 3. Business Objectives

1. Reduce the time required to collect and organize company documents.
2. Reduce the time required to identify important information in filings.
3. Provide consistent comparisons between current and historical disclosures.
4. Create a centralized research workspace for each company.
5. Provide evidence-linked answers rather than unsupported model responses.
6. Generate reusable professional-quality research reports.
7. Establish an architecture suitable for enterprise security, scalability, and governance.
8. Create a foundation for future valuation, portfolio, alerting, and collaboration features.

### Target outcomes

- Reduce first-pass research time by at least 60%.
- Retrieve supported SEC filings within 15 minutes of detection under normal operating conditions.
- Provide citations for at least 95% of material factual AI claims.
- Reach at least 98% ingestion success for supported document formats.
- Reach at least 95% accuracy for audited core financial metrics.
- Support at least five years of filing history per company workspace.
- Return common research-question responses within 15 seconds at p95, excluding long-running report generation.
- Generate a standard research report within five minutes after required data is available.
- Maintain an audit trail for ingestion, processing, model execution, user actions, and generated artifacts.

## 4. Product Principles

### 4.1 Evidence before interpretation
Every material factual statement generated by the platform must be associated with source evidence whenever a reliable source exists.

### 4.2 Facts and analysis remain separate
The platform must distinguish reported facts, calculated values, management statements, AI interpretations, analyst opinions, forecasts, and recommendations.

### 4.3 Human review remains authoritative
AI may support research conclusions but must not present generated investment opinions as guaranteed outcomes.

### 4.4 Reproducibility
Important AI outputs must record model, prompt version, retrieval context, source documents, processing version, and generation timestamp.

### 4.5 Security and privacy by design
Authentication, authorization, tenant isolation, logging, secrets management, secure defaults, and operational controls are mandatory from the beginning.

### 4.6 Modularity
Retrieval, extraction, search, analytics, AI orchestration, reporting, and user interfaces must remain separable components.

### 4.7 Financial-domain correctness
The platform must account for fiscal calendars, restatements, unit scaling, currency, GAAP/non-GAAP measures, segment changes, acquisitions, divestitures, stock splits, taxonomy changes, and amended filings.

## 8. Core User Journeys

### 8.1 Company onboarding
1. User enters ticker, company name, or CIK.
2. System resolves the security and legal entity.
3. Ambiguity is displayed when necessary.
4. User opens or creates the company workspace.
5. Historical supported documents are retrieved.
6. Processing status is displayed.
7. Dashboard sections become available as processing completes.

### 8.2 Latest-quarter review
1. User opens a company workspace.
2. System identifies the latest completed reporting period.
3. User reviews highlights, financial changes, commentary, guidance, risks, and catalysts.
4. Every material statement links to source evidence.
5. User compares one or more historical periods.
6. User asks follow-up questions or creates research notes.
7. User optionally generates a report.

### 8.3 Document investigation
1. User selects a filing or source document.
2. System displays original and normalized content.
3. User searches within the document.
4. Extracted facts and sections are visible.
5. Selecting an AI-generated claim navigates to its supporting passage.

### 8.4 Research question answering
1. User asks a company-specific question.
2. System classifies the question.
3. Structured facts and source passages are retrieved.
4. Evidence is reranked and validated.
5. Answer distinguishes facts, calculations, management claims, and interpretation.
6. Citations are attached.
7. Uncertainty and limitations are communicated.

### 8.5 Research-report generation
1. User selects company, period, report type, and comparison periods.
2. System freezes an approved source snapshot.
3. Structured facts and narrative evidence are retrieved.
4. Draft sections are generated.
5. Citation and consistency checks are run.
6. User edits and reviews the report.
7. Approval workflow is completed where required.
8. Report is exported as a versioned artifact.

## 11. AI and Model Requirements

### Grounding
- Use retrieved evidence.
- Make source passages reviewable.
- Avoid unsupported assertions.
- Refuse or qualify answers when evidence is insufficient.

### Prompt governance
- Version-control prompts.
- Review prompt changes.
- Associate prompt versions with outputs.
- Require evaluation gates before production promotion.

### Model governance
- Maintain an approved model list.
- Keep providers replaceable through abstraction.
- Allow task-specific model routing.
- Provide provider-outage fallback behavior.

### Evaluation
Maintain evaluation datasets for citation correctness, financial extraction, filing classification, guidance extraction, risk detection, comparison, hallucination detection, numerical consistency, answer usefulness, report quality, and prompt-injection resistance.

### Financial reasoning safeguards
The AI must verify fiscal periods, preserve units/currencies, recalculate arithmetic when needed, avoid mixing GAAP/non-GAAP, avoid incompatible period comparisons, identify conflicts/missing data, distinguish company guidance from analyst forecasts, and separate historical facts from forward-looking statements.

## 12. Data Lineage

Every derived artifact must be traceable through:

1. Original source.
2. Retrieved file.
3. Normalized document.
4. Document section.
5. Extracted fact/passage.
6. Transformation/calculation.
7. Model execution.
8. Generated answer/report.
9. Human modification.
10. Final approved artifact.

Lineage records include timestamps, processing versions, methods, and responsible components.

## 13. Legal and Compliance Requirements

Formal review is required for SEC usage rules, investor-relations terms, transcript licensing, audio rights, market-data licensing, copyright/redistribution restrictions, AI-provider terms, record retention, financial disclosures, research-publication requirements, and jurisdiction-specific investment-advice constraints.

No production source is enabled without an approved rights classification.

## 14. Audit Requirements

Audit authentication, permission changes, source changes, model changes, prompt changes, retrieval events, processing failures, user questions, generated answers, report generation, report edits, approvals, exports, administrative actions, and security events.

Audit data must be protected from unauthorized modification.
